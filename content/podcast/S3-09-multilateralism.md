I don't know that I would call it an aberration in the sense that I don't think that after Donald Trump leaves the presidency, the US will suddenly revert to some kind of pre-Trump norm. I do think the extremity of the unilateralism will abate; I think future US administrations will understand the value of alliances and will understand the value of partnerships and will understand the value of diplomacy. And I think they will start to reinvest in those things. They will have a very, very tough time to rebuild those relationships.
 
What does this mean for the multilateral system? I think as you say, Luke, I wouldn't even call it multipolar. I think it will actually become a lot more genuinely multilateral in the sense that we will see a diminution of the dominance of the really big players. I think it will be empowering to the middle powers and the smaller countries, because I think what this period has demonstrated and is demonstrating is that collective action can be powerful. And that, just as workers collectivising into unions were able to transform themselves from a very unequal relationship with their employers into a powerful force that was able to negotiate better conditions, I think the smaller countries of the world are learning that they can work together in their mutual interests to generate the kinds of benefits that international cooperation can deliver, preferably with the cooperation of the major powers, but, if necessary, in their absence.
